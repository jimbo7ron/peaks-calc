Assumptions & Methodology

Climbing Speed

Based on power-to-weight physics: Speed = Power × 0.92 ÷ (Weight × 9.81 × (Gradient + 0.004))

  • 8% drivetrain losses — accounts for chain, bearings, tyre deformation
  • Rolling resistance (Crr) — 0.004 (typical road tyres on good tarmac)
  • Gradient — uses average gradient per segment (doesn't model steep sections within)

Flat & Descent Speed

  • Flats: ~32 km/h baseline at 180W, scales with power (cube root relationship)
  • Descents: Conservative 38-50 km/h depending on gradient (safety-limited, not power-limited)

W/kg Calculation

Rider weight estimated as system weight minus 10kg (bike ~8kg + shoes, bottles, spares ~2kg)

What's NOT Modelled

  • Fatigue — you'll go slower on later climbs; adjust "Climbing Power" to your expected average
  • Weather — wind, heat, rain all affect times significantly
  • Drafting — group riding on flats can be faster than modelled
  • Steep sections — WTF Corner (17%) and other steep ramps within segments

Calibration

Model validated against actual 2025 Peaks Challenge finish data. At 210W climbing / 170W flat / 82kg system weight, predicts 8:31 vs actual 8:34 (3 min difference).
